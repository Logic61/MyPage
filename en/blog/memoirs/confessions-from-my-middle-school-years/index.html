    Confessions from My Middle School Years

    Apr 30, 2022 Shane Lorien

    It is regrettable how quickly time passes; in the blink of an eye, I have said goodbye to junior high.

    I still remember going up to the high floors for the first time, gazing at the sea, with white clouds and blue skies, and tall towers and low buildings. If it rained or was foggy, the view was misty, as if the whole world was only this small island, with nothing but the vast blue sky and the sea beyond. If the rain and fog were any thicker, everything outside the gymnasium would be indistinct, immersed in a haze, which was quite interesting.

    Although the food in the cafeteria was not great, it carries many memories. Every morning, I had to carefully consider whether to eat steamed buns or steamed bread. We had soup for lunch and afternoon. If there was meat, I would often take half a bowl of meat. Once, the cafeteria cooked fish balls, and several classmates almost finished half of the fish balls. Looking back, I feel so naive, but at the time, I found it very fun. Every time we finished soup, we would compare whose meat was more, until we rushed to the cafeteria every day after school just to get the first bowl of soup, and thankfully, I never fell. Our class always sat in the center, and the teachers often walked by, occasionally giving us snacks. Although the things were light, they came from the teachers, and thus brought us good moods.

    I happened to live in a large dormitory, which was very spacious, allowing us to move around often. We also played illegal activities like playing ground ping-pong and kicking soccer against the wall with the neighbors. In the upper grade of junior high, we were given glow sticks. More than ten or twenty people crowded into the adjacent dormitory to practice fencing, waving the sticks in the dark. There was one student who was very skilled, who dominated the three-point area without losing ground, drawing cheers. After playing for a while, the sticks were almost all broken. Even after the lights were fixed, we could still play. Physics taught electricity, so the lights became our little white rabbits. We explored the change in their colors, explored series and parallel connections, and explored what could conduct electricity. However, it would have been simpler to just use the experiment box. When the student manager said it was a club activity, a few of us gathered together and played. We placed the electric motor on the ground, supplied electricity, and adjusted the angle, and it could walk by itself; we put the wires in the water, and nothing happened… There were countless such instances.

    I also picked up some things earlier that could be used for flying discs, which I used to train my and my classmates’ reaction abilities in the dormitory, exercise our hand muscles, and enhance our combat ability using props and terrain advantages. Later, if the disc wasn’t thrown fast enough, it could be caught very easily, which was somewhat useful. We also played with flying cards, but it made the card owner unhappy, so the broken school ID cards came in handy. However, because of their danger, we only used them for hitting metal or peeling apples, not for war. Later, I learned the technique of throwing the school ID card with bare hands into an apple, which suggests that with a school ID card in hand, one could defend oneself.

    When gathered in the dormitory, we naturally played all kinds of games: Werewolf, Who is the Mole, script-based role-playing… Fortunately, I was only caught once. Unfortunately, that time, someone from another dormitory came over to play, and I almost got kicked out. Fortunately, my attitude was sincere, and I confessed and was treated leniently, so it was a scare but no danger. As for playing cards, we almost never got caught at noon or in the afternoon. If we were still not at ease, we could open our suitcases and play inside, or simply call for someone to guard the perimeter while we played cards. However, it was hard to play cards in the afternoon because we were sleepy.

    Later, I got into standing exercises. At noon, I went with good classmates to stand in the Yinghuai Pavilion. If you go to Yinghuai Pavilion, it’s on a small path to the right after entering through the East Gate. The soccer class seemed to often go that way to the teaching building in the morning. After standing for about half a month, it was good for nourishing the spirit, but I was still worried about being kicked out of the dormitory. Although I was not discovered, later I gradually started standing in the dormitory or stopped altogether.

    At noon, because the teachers delayed classes, or because we were tired of the cafeteria food, some classmates always ate instant noodles in the dormitory. Every time, it was like stealing, carrying hot water in a kettle and running back to the dormitory. The student manager would come to look, and we hid in all sorts of places, and almost never got caught. Now there are places selling instant noodles downstairs from the dormitory, and we eat instant noodles for meals in the dormitory every day, which is a huge contrast. If I were to say where the fun was, it would be in the dormitory. Wandering inside and outside the rules, coming and going from the security office, and reminiscing endlessly.

    The classroom was also very fun. For a period of time, there was a fixed activity before evening self-study—taking photos. Taking all kinds of expressions and saving the screenshots to look back on. Before evening self-study, I was basically always playing ball. Every time I entered, I would accidentally take a picture. Seeing others’ bizarre poses, don’t laugh right away; it’s best to cover your mouth with clothes or wear a mask before laughing, otherwise, you might be next. Of course, if you don’t care, you don’t have to. Laughing for ten minutes and starting evening self-study in a cheerful mood was quite efficient. Especially since the second middle school had less homework, which could be finished in a normal class period, or even before self-study, allowing us to study independently and develop interests, which could be called very free.

    The teachers were all very interesting. The chemistry teacher, who usually looked serious, showed a different side during evening self-study. She wrote on the blackboard, Mg + ZnSO₄ = Zn + MgSO₄ — Your beauty stole my heart, and we saw a side of chemical romance. The Chinese teacher always said, “You’ve forgotten it all in Java, have you?” Because of the continuous reading of “have you?”, it became “sa”, and so it was turned by classmates into “sha”. She was usually quite serious in class, but when chatting with classmates privately, she was very amiable. I still remember once going to her because she dropped something, and she cried out like a little girl, “Oh dear.” This shows a lot.

    The math teacher was originally Teacher Liu, who took us to see the stars many times, using his telescope to let us appreciate the splendor of the universe. After finishing the main lesson in class, he would say, “The main feature starts,” and tell us anecdotes about various scientists. At the same time, under his guidance, our class’s math also improved. Unfortunately, he was later transferred to Middle School No. 1. Then it was Teacher Shi’s turn, whose Northeastern accent also brought us a lot of fun, and classmates competed to imitate her accent. She often gave small rewards, like rainbow candies, lollipops, and planet cups, sometimes giving several at once.

    The English teacher’s class atmosphere was also very relaxed, and our class’s English scores were also very cheerful in a joyful atmosphere. For history, there were two teachers: Teacher Kang, who was amiable, and Teacher Ge, who was humorous and witty. Each had its own strengths and were all very good. The geography teacher was serious and rigorous, and the biology teacher always said to ask classmates to stand at the back, but never did. The two politics teachers created an open classroom atmosphere, and the physical teacher’s good temper and chicken legs…

    All these things, none of them are lacking. Junior high life was very colorful, very relaxed, and very interesting.

    The island embraced by the sea and blue sky, the campus surrounded by green trees and red flowers, how beautiful, how leisurely.
