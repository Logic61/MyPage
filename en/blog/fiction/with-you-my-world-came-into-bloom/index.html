    With You, My World Came into Bloom

    Jan 14, 2021 Shane Lorien

    Still Xu Zhimo, still youthful, romantic literature, but I never thought that I would have an OI experience in junior high, three years of physics in high school, and then return to computer science in college. I regret slacking off during the OI training back then (

    His body was still like a tender shoot, graceful, like a faint flame of wax, like the gentle brilliance of a morning star. The speaker was not immune to being surprised by the weight of his breath, and thought that this was the divine transformation of something delicate and faint. —“Man Shu Fei’er”

    I never imagined that there could be such a flower, such a spring scene, in my world.

    The rain was drizzling, and I reluctantly stepped into the gate of the information training camp—a place of forced attendance. For a bright future, I first had to sacrifice these few days, from 8 AM to 4 PM. I looked at the seating chart, searching for a corner to sit in. The environment was a bit dim, much like my mood. But at that moment, a girl walked over and struck me—as if a fluorescent light had suddenly turned on while you were rubbing your sleepy eyes; or as if a clap of thunder suddenly exploded right next to your ears. She was slender, with a pale face, as if ill, but her eyes shimmered with starlight, like an attractive black hole that made it hard to look away. If you saw her, the first word that would pop into your mind is—clear, the clarity of water, the purity of moonlight; or else, it is—natural, as if born, without any trace of artificial pretense, just simple and pure, yet infinitely charming, like rivers and mountains. Her beauty is a holistic beauty, a complete beauty; her entire being exudes an ethereal grace. But if you wanted to describe it in detail, you wouldn’t know where to start, and could only use some general phrases to roughly depict your feelings and her silhouette—the words learned in the human world are difficult to describe my state of mind, and difficult to describe the myriad aspects of her beauty.

    “Hello.” She spoke first, sitting down next to me. Her voice was like a singing nightingale, slowly uttering two notes that lightly struck my heart. I was slightly stunned, then replied, “You—good.” She smiled faintly and then turned her gaze to the teacher. Class started.

    At first, I thought the content was quite interesting because I could barely follow the explanations. But as time passed, those lines of lengthy code tripped me up, and I couldn’t solve a single example problem. I looked at her with a hint of embarrassment, but she had already finished, leisurely browsing the problems. She noticed my expression and smiled, saying, “This is your first time in this kind of class, right?” I nodded. She then put down the problem in her hand and patiently explained the thought process to me. The foggy problem gradually revealed its outline and was finally conquered. Later, even when the answer was right before my eyes, I still found it difficult to understand, so she explained the meaning line by line. I worried about wasting her time, but she smiled and quoted an ancient phrase: “Teaching benefits both parties. Helping you is also beneficial to me.” So I accepted this kindness and stopped refusing, enjoying this spring-like warmth and beauty.

    In the afternoon, something astonishing happened. She saw the bright red error message popping up in my compiler and said with a tone of regret, “It’s angry.” I looked at her with surprise, but I couldn’t find any trace of joking. In her eyes, the code and the compiler were alive. “They are alive, like sprites, so cute,” she added, gazing blankly at the complicated code. I still had a hint of doubt, but she continued, “Everything has a spirit; it’s just that the spirits we call life need to be more obvious.”

    This day, which I thought would be boring, passed so pleasantly.

    She was like a dream, and also like a bubble, and dreams and bubbles always disappear too quickly. I had met people like this on the internet: protecting me everywhere while playing games, patiently chatting with my naive self, but then vanishing the next day; and there were also neighbors like an older sister, who moved away after only living with me for a few days. Although these experiences might seem immature or ridiculous, they repeatedly confirmed one thing—beautiful things are always easy to lose. This made me worry that she would leave the next day.

    But the next day, she still appeared, like a dream, still sitting next to me. The third, fourth, and fifth days were the same. I felt as if I was always bathed in the warm sunlight and fragrance of spring. Until the sixth day, she coughed and inexplicably brought up blood. That bright red blood splashed on the paper, but it poured onto my heart. Facing my astonished and worried gaze, she still managed to maintain that smile, sorrowful and melancholic, and shook her head, saying it was nothing. The lesson that day was the same, but I could no longer concentrate. When class ended, she looked at the sky and suddenly said, “Life is beautiful, but very few people realize it.” After a pause, as if remembering something, she said to me, “You, remember to be happy.”. Combined with the bloodstain from before, a terrifying guess surfaced in my heart, but it was covered by endless doubt and confusion.

    The next day, she didn’t come.

    Between the restart of the class and the file update, a pop-up window appeared on her computer. She said she had returned to her hometown, so there was no need to worry; she said she had cancer and little time left, and in these last days, she wanted to see this world with her family and relatives; she said this world is very beautiful and good, and I must cherish it. She attended this training camp only to touch the world of code she loved one last time at the end of her life; unexpectedly, she met me, someone who had almost no foundation but came to take an advanced class. She thought that since she had never been a teacher, she would use this to make up for the gap. She also said that I was quite interesting, at least in her view, so she wrote this letter as a farewell. She didn’t know my name, but still wished me a good life and wished me all my wishes come true.

    All her previously confusing actions now have an explanation. But I would rather never know. This truth is too cruel. Must beautiful things be destined to wither early? I had just touched the light and warmth of spring, and now I see it fading, it’s too tragic.

    But she still left something—a seedling, a seedling of beauty and hope that took root in my heart. It is still tender, but it already emits light and warmth, dispelling the darkness in my spiritual world, like the warm sun of early spring, and like the sun in the dark night. From then on, whenever the wave of darkness struck, it shone once after another, illuminating me and giving me the courage to move forward.

    Slowly, I finally understood her seemingly inscrutable words. Everything has a spirit; every blade of grass, every tree, every fallen leaf and flower, exudes its own beauty, or the beauty of life, or the beauty of fading, and countless beauties hidden deep in life, just waiting to be perceived. The seedling finally grew and bloomed, beauty and hope intertwined within it, fixing my world in spring. I suddenly found that everything was so beautiful; I felt as if I were in an ocean of hope. Although she has left, she has never truly left; she has moved into my heart, appearing when I think of her, appearing when darkness comes. This beauty, as long as I am here, it will be here, and it will never wither.

    I know this is your blooming. That beauty and hope are your light. You forged this eternal brightness, igniting the undying spring in my heart. Although I do not know your name, I still want to thank you—thank you for this unspeakable kindness—you let my world bloom in spring.
