    Shadows of a Dream, II

    Mar 22, 2026 Shane Lorien

    Guess this was all bottled up (

    Dusk was coming on. The dim yellow sunlight spilled lazily over everything.

    The park we’d meant to visit was still a fair way off. We’d been held up earlier, and now it was getting late. Should we go? After giving it some thought, we decided to leave it to fate. If we could squeeze onto the next bus, we’d go. Otherwise we’d each head home.

    Just as we set out for the bus stop, a bus drove past us. One look told us it was packed. I was still keen, though. I wanted a proper wander, even if it was late. I called to the others and ran straight for the stop before anyone could answer. Luckily, one or two people got off, opening up a little of the precious space at the front, and I got on. I looked back. The others were still some way behind. Maybe they’d already decided to call it a day.

    Then someone came running up to the door. It was her, a friend I knew fairly well. She hurried aboard and squeezed in beside me in the yellow area where, supposedly, we weren’t allowed to stand. We managed to tap our cards, and the bus pulled gently away. There wasn’t much room in the yellow area to begin with. Now there were three of us in it. The stranger stood on one side, I stood on the other, and she was between us. She glanced at me and shifted a little closer. I took a breath, as I was already doing, and caught a faint fragrance. On a bus, I suppose that counts as a luxury.

    Feeling her warmth against me, I asked, “So it’s just the two of us going now?”

    “Yeah. The others are going home.”

    …

    The bus braked, and she came straight into my arms. “Are you, are you okay?” I asked. She shook her head but stayed where she was, leaning against me, one hand on the same yellow rail behind the door that I was holding. When I looked down I could see only the top of her head, and her hair falling over her shoulder. There was that light fragrance. Every now and then the bus changed speed and brought us a little closer together. The last of the sun and the evening clouds, streetlights and traffic racing past outside, the rumble of the bus underway … Neither of us said a word, and I felt a strange peace, a strange contentment.

    Two more stops would take us to the sea. A few more after that, when the bus turned inland, we’d reach the park we’d planned to visit. She asked, “Do you really want to go to that park?”

    “Not especially.”

    “Then why did you run for the bus by yourself?”

    “I just wanted to wander a bit more. I hardly ever get out for a proper day like this.”

    “It’ll be dark any minute. Are we still going? What if it’s closed?”

    “Oh, right. I forgot about that. Hmm … shall we get off by the sea and look at the sunset?”

    “Sure.”

    When we got off, we looked at each other. Thinking about what had just happened, I grew a little shy and turned my head toward the water in the distance. Then I heard footsteps. She walked slowly into my line of sight, tipped her head a little to look at me, and held a smile at the corner of her mouth. Just as I was wondering what to do, she held out her hand. I stood there for a moment, looked at her again, and slowly took it. Our fingers interlaced. Her hand was warm and soft. We walked along the shore like that, hand in hand, taking our time. The sea breeze was gentle, and the sky was flushed as though heaven had had too much wine. We said very little. We’d already found what lay at the bottom of our hearts. When night fell we stopped, and finished the poem with an embrace.

    We held our palms against each other’s to compare. “My fingers are a whole knuckle longer than yours,” I said, teasing.

    “So what?”

    “I suppose it doesn’t mean anything.”

    “Idiot. It means you’ve got to look after me.”
