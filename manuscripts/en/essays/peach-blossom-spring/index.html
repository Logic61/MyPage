Mention Peach Blossom Spring, and people are bound to think of hiding in the hills and woods and never coming back.

When I look for Peach Blossom Spring, I am looking for a way to keep my heart in Peach Blossom Spring. There need be no “retreat by the hillside,” no “sleeping alone and waking to sing.”

That makes this very world Peach Blossom Spring.
“If the feeling in me is true and fair,”
any place will do.

The slanting sun on grass and trees, an ordinary lane: here is Peach Blossom Spring.

People come and go along the long street,
all bustle and laughter.
Children get together and run and jump at their games,
blue sky and white clouds drifting above.

You are among the streets where people live,
and what you feel
is their simple kindness.

I still remember—

  • When a bent old woman comes shuffling by, there is always a young person who goes up to steady her and have a laugh with her.
  • When the children go door to door looking for somewhere to play, whoever opens it is happy to set out fruit and snacks.
  • When someone takes the car out, they always take the neighbor who’s going the same way along for a comfortable ride, free of charge …

A little bridge, running water, the houses of a village: here is Peach Blossom Spring.

Birds lodge upon the roof;
water circles below the hall.

The man of the house works in the fields.
A child runs through the flowers after a butterfly.

Here you can follow a narrow path, walk beside a clear stream, and find how beautiful people can be to one another.

I remember, too—

  • Wherever the old man goes after picking vegetables, he gives someone a share. The reason is always the same: “Can’t eat them all.”
  • Children tiptoe into the field to “steal” peanuts, beans, or something else to eat. So long as they take very little, the owners let it be. The parents send something afterward to make up for it. The owners refuse it every time …

A retreat by the hillside, with room for a great soul.

Why shouldn’t an uninhabited stretch of mountains, woods, and meadow be a Peach Blossom Spring of its own?

Clear water and flourishing trees,
high mountains and thick grass.
There is plenty to delight in.

The river goes east;
the passing clouds lean on the wind.

There is no village kindness here.
There is the quiet grace of the land itself.

In the morning—
birds singing at first light, a cock crowing the day in.

In the evening—
mountains high and the moon small, stones showing where the water has gone down.

At dusk—
the tipsy sun
drags down its flushed face
and dyes half the sky red.

When the wind rises—
a clear breeze comes slowly and no wave troubles the water.
Or waves cross the river a thousand feet high, and ten thousand bamboo stems bend.

When rain falls—
wind and rain whisper and thunder rolls.
Everything puts on a thin veil of rain
and takes on a new and misty charm.

Birds and wild things,
trees, flowers, and grasses.
How they can make you lose yourself.

The lanes of a busy town,
the houses of the countryside,
mountains and woods and wild grass …

Look where you will, and there is no Peach Blossom Spring.
And yet where isn’t it Peach Blossom Spring?

With your heart in Peach Blossom Spring,
where could it be otherwise?
