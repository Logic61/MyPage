I got free of the sea of suffering and broke through the dream.
Though darkness still sits over the whole of the sky,
I know there is light, and there is hope.

— Epigraph

Lost and at sea, cold and hushed, dim and dark, heavy and faint.

That was the world I lived in.
Simple. Darkness, plain and simple.

Oh, that night that had taken root so quietly, back when I was a child watching cartoons. It came rearing in like a horse off its reins, a bird with its tether snapped, in too much haste, too thick and heavy, rolling right over me.

The tide of darkness raised wave on wave, beating in, beating in, beating in …

The inch of island under my feet had long been near breaking. Now, with the force of the tide behind it, it broke all at once. It sank. It vanished. And I fell, and sank, and kept on falling, down into the boundless sea of suffering. I really had seen the dream for the dream it was. And what of it? There was not a sliver of light through that dark.

Darkness wears away the mind. It always leaves a person lost.

In the tide I searched in a panic for something that might save my life and make my spirit whole. A length of dead wood was enough to send me floundering after it, though experience had taught me how quickly such things were gone.

I could hardly hold back that fever in my heart, the urge to follow this faint hope. If the dream broke, what harm could there be? So try it. Just try it.

I don’t know how many times. Once, and once, and once more.

The sad thing was that they all broke in just the same way:

A little crack. A little more. And then, clean through.

Nothing new about that, is there?
And how it hurt.

Imagine taking that little flame of hope with you, time after time, going out to touch the wild tide of darkness. Before long the dark water beats down the frail flame. It goes out, it scatters, it gives no more light.

One touch of Death’s breath and the flame is out and gone, hardly to be seen again. It has become something for the darkness to feed on.

Darkness has no face, but I could almost see it grin.

Ah.

The darkness blotted out sky and earth. To me it was worse than any great flood. How wretched it was.
The world had put out the light. Darkness sat over the whole of the sky, over this boundless sea of suffering.

Overcast, overcast.

So I put on a veil. I might almost have been part of the darkness myself, there in the dark stirring up its mud and raising its waves.

The water rises here, and the mask smiles;
A boat goes over there, and the mask claps.

Oh, the pitiful, ridiculous song that goes on in the dark and wears a person away.
Let it melt into the darkness. There is some idle amusement there, at least.

Yes, idle amusement.

Tease the frightened fish in the dark and watch them dart every which way.
A moment’s pleasure. Perhaps enough moments add up to something that lasts.

Let the rest of the time be painful if it will.
I want even a little while of pleasure—
Pleasure, a poor and pointless consolation.

Look, another piece of wood. It doesn’t look dead, at any rate.

Who can tell what it’s like inside?
I’ve seen wood that looked solid and turned out rotten before.

Did I dare hope? I did.
And it never did any good.

Experience could tell me how it would go. A scrap of light might seem to cling to it and touch something in me, but it was no use, never any use.

A scrap of light breaks easily,
more brittle than dry grass.
One lick of the fire of darkness and it is ash.

But you know how rare even that moment of pleasure is.

Well. I’ll be greedy for one more moment.

So I reached out and touched the light.

It was whole.

It was the whole of the light.
It was light with a soul.

True light has a soul, of course. I’d never dared hope I’d touch true light.
And there I was, touching it. What a wonder!

The moment I touched it, the light spread out in a wave. It drove the darkness back from me and swept away the boundless sea of suffering.

Ah!
How could these lumbering words tell one ten-thousandth of what I felt then?

What the rain brings after a long drought is hardly something these rough sentences can say.

It was a little like this:

  • Someone thirsty for days coming upon water bright with light
  • Someone tired from a whole day’s work finally lying down in a soft bed
  • Someone hungry for who knows how long at last eating a hot meal

You had already given up every hope,
and then hope comes straight into your arms.

Even an atheist can’t help but sigh, a little dazed:

“Thank God.”

Before the gladness had passed, the fear came up again.

I had come across a light like this before.
Sometimes it stayed a day or two. A few weeks at the very longest. Then there wasn’t a trace of it.

I bathed in the light and worried all the while.

One week.
Two weeks.
Time ran by.

She hadn’t faded.
She was still there.

One month.
Two months.

She still stood beside me.

The fears and defenses in my heart slowly fell away.
It seemed I could be sure at last.
Here was light that was truly, truly everlasting.

Suddenly.

The light dimmed a little.

She grew a little dim,
then a little dimmer,
and then, altogether dark.

Death had come again.
The same trick, the same sight.
The pain a hundred times, ten thousand times as great!

Ah!

If I couldn’t have her always,
why give me those two months?

All that certainty, all that conviction, all that forever.
How absurd.

The voice of Darkness broke like thunder in my head:

“You were wrong.”

The voice of Death broke like thunder in my head:

“You were wrong.”

The voice of Light broke like thunder in my head:

“You were wrong.”

All I could do, dumbfounded, was ask:

“Why?”

All three spoke at once:

“We are here. We have always been here.”

And then I saw, at the bottom of my heart, three colors shining through one another.

Darkness.
Death.
Light.

They mingled strangely and gave off light.

I stared in wonder.
Could the three of them be …?

At once the three answered:

“We are one.”
