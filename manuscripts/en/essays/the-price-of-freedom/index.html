Essay

Freedom, during high school, was a star-like, dazzling state for me. However, when I actually gained the freedom to act, I felt a slight nostalgia for the former days, when I was merely naive and just went with the flow.

Before entering university, I thought there might be various group activities, and I would get to know some people. But the freedom at school was truly so; there were almost no mandatory group activities except for military training. Well, I joined a club, thinking I could practice the piano every day and meet people, but I couldn’t practice because I wasn’t in the performance department. I guess my rendition of the ‘Celebration’ from Beethoven was too bad… Then, I didn’t really want to go to their performance lectures or anything. The most I went to was the guiding and Tai Chi club, but because my social skills are really weak, I can only say that I recognized a few senior students. I added one person on WeChat and exchanged zero words; it’s so abstract (and maybe I could have met people through the student work office, but I signed up for the Youth Federation, thinking I wouldn’t join the student council, but the Youth Federation was like joining it anyway. I missed the most essential class on computer skills, and everything after that felt meaningless, so I didn’t go much, nor did I join any departments.)

Freedom means that one must bear everything oneself. High degrees of freedom mean a higher ceiling, but they can also bring a lower floor. The people I can talk to the most now are basically my roommates and the people I meet sporadically in class. I feel it’s a bit too much like being a mouse.

Socializing is like this, and personal development is similar. Having lost the mandatory Chinese language class, although I still have a strong desire to do some related learning, I am constantly dragged along by calculus and linear algebra. The priority of language has been lowered significantly; I only occasionally read poetry, and besides online novels and sporadically read poems and Ci, I haven’t read much. This really feels indefensible. Books are indeed supplements to the spirit. After a long time of not reading, when my heart is agitated with emotions, floating in confusion and melancholy that is hard to dispel, I finally turn to the pen, but I still have nothing to write. I don’t know how to express it, which makes me feel deeply miserable.

Tearing down the high walls of constraint, freedom bends down and lets in temptations as vast as the sea. Short videos, etc., are certainly legitimate as temporary entertainment, but alas, time flies. Subjectively, it feels like an instant, but objectively, an unknown amount of time has already passed. When time passes silently, I spend a considerable part of my life in repeatedly sighing and racing against the remaining precious time.

I should pursue what I want freely and operate actively and efficiently. Whether it is seeking the tranquility among flowers and grass, or wandering through famous mountains and rivers, or dominating an academic field, or ruling a game—all of these can be granted to me by freedom. However, when confusion envelops freedom, freedom turns into poison. In confusion, one throws oneself into the immediately gratifying pleasures that are within reach, gaining illusory joy from repeated instant feedback, only to feel emptiness and confusion upon waking. Even if there is a goal, even a simple small goal, it is still prone to being overshadowed by external things due to low execution ability, and in a flash, it is already sunset.

Over the past semester of university, I have gradually felt that I have lost the ability to concentrate for long periods. In high school, I could play games for a whole day, but I could also study all day. When I got home, I would still ponder the solution to a math problem. Now? The things like ‘divided attention’ mentioned in the DS essays in high school have actually happened to me; it’s laughable. The easily accessible, lightweight temptations bring enormous influence.
