— An old piece, written for an assignment that unexpectedly brought a friendship to mind. It was young, and reading it over, I hardly know what to feel first. —

Mid-Autumn again, another year gone by;
quietly, the autumn moon grows round.

Did Li Bai ever wonder
whether the bright moon that drank with him
would look the same a thousand years from then?

I’ll tuck a few mooncakes up my sleeve
and take them to Wangshu.
Perhaps she’ll leave her clear light in our world
and help us drive the coronavirus past the nine heavens.

I recite “Plantain” to the moon
and invite her to wander these fields with me.
I wonder
whether she’d stoop to pluck and gather plantain,
whether she’d care to see how bright our flowers bloom.

Between the clouds and the moon
I catch a little of the moon-toad’s light.
It must be Li Bai’s wine,
spilled along the edge of clouds the immortals crumbled in their hands.

People long ago hid fine wine in their bamboo slips,
something sweet for the spirit to savor.
Clear light flickers in the wine, and the white moon sinks and rises.
It gives us strength to scatter the dark
and joy enough to watch the moon grow full, year after year.

Written at Mid-Autumn, 2020.
