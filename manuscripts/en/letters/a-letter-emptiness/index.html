Writing a letter to a stranger that isn’t short, the reason is probably obvious. In my clumsy view, it can only be categorized as novelty and loneliness. Writing letters is probably a rare thing in this fast-paced era. Even electronic mail seems to carry the flavor of a simpler time, giving a sense of novelty. Furthermore, in this life, it is hard to find friends with whom one can vent freely, or to whom one can express hesitation or reluctance. Casting one’s gaze onto the internet, sending a letter to an unknown address that speaks of the self but doesn’t reveal too much, seems tempting.

I don’t even know why, but when I was happy, I wrote all the above. Actually, I just wanted to say that I plan to talk about my recent reflections on feeling empty due to my own atomization.

I just finished a year at university and am preparing for my sophomore year. Below are excerpts from my diary. However, since I set up a blog to upload them, even if no one visits, I finally have a sense of readership, even though most of it is just talking to myself:

A semester flew by, and I’m back home. I feel like even after a year, I haven’t shaken off the lingering emptiness after the college entrance exams. The night after taking the last final exam, I played some Mahjong (Jianhun) for over two hours and got two stars. Today, on the way back, I read a few chapters of a novel, and after getting home, I scrolled through social media… and felt empty.

So how can I avoid feeling empty? When studying, whether it’s math class or major courses, although I’m not very clear about my long-term goals or what I want to do in the future, I don’t feel empty. This might be related to what is called meritocracy. However, such brief satisfaction is like water from no source; before finding a spring, one can only achieve such temporary satisfaction. At least I can find pleasure in the derivation process, and I can tell myself that I am improving my abilities, which makes me feel less empty.

But I don’t have the motivation to study, nor the motivation to study continuously. If there are no deadlines or exams. I am willing to learn, but I can’t start, and I can’t continue. This is difficult. So what can I start? I’m actually too lazy to start games, but I can play them for longer. The easiest thing to start is probably social media, QQ, VX, Xiaohongshu, or Tonghuashun (?). Even novels. I still need to open the app, then click on the library, and select and open one. With social media, I just need to enter the app, and various pieces of information are immediately visible. But although it’s easy to start, it’s ultimately meaningless, so I can’t scroll for long. But the boredom doesn’t last long, so after being bored, maybe I look at a couple of PPTs, look at a couple of books, and see some difficult parts, and then automatically pick up my phone to scroll through social media. I know I am distracting myself, interrupting my learning of knowledge in difficulty, but I am powerless to resist. What motivation do I have to move forward? I don’t know what reason I have to refuse short-term temptations, because I cannot see the future.

Looking to history for lessons is probably a good choice. In high school, I could indeed study all day. The motivation mentioned earlier was very strong: getting into a good university, or even just scoring higher than classmates. Moreover, everyone was studying, and the atmosphere was great. Besides that, when it comes to things that distract my attention, there is also an activation cost, which is not much different from the activation cost of studying, like looking at magazines, or writing some fiction or articles.

So what can I emulate? I can’t find motivation, and I don’t know how to find it. The atmosphere, though, is something I can indeed learn from, and I have actually practiced it. Studying at the library, I can almost study all day, and then go back at night to play games. I think this is completely enough. It’s just that I don’t have classmates like in high school to study and exchange with. My roommates seem to be in their own worlds. We only occasionally complain after exams, and there’s no idea for daily interaction. When I encounter something mysterious while studying advanced math or linear algebra, I still tend to look for high school classmates to complain to, but now that I’m studying major courses, there are no familiar high school classmates for me to complain to. As for restricting my temptations, it can indeed be achieved by turning on ‘Do Not Disturb,’ and it does have a certain effect.

But motivation, I feel it is still the most fundamental thing. So I have to start trying. This semester, I applied for a school-enterprise collaborative project and unexpectedly passed. Having something relatively clear to do, with clear requirements and clear acceptance standards, is actually similar to the effect of an exam deadline. Although it’s a bit funny, this thing can urge me to do something substantial. Specifically, it’s about the intention of dog breeds through voiceprint and posture recognition, which is indeed quite interesting. AI also provided a very clear path of separate training followed by multi-modal fusion. I just need to follow and do it, and I should be able to grow my knowledge in the entire process of audio and image processing, as well as neural network training. I feel like it’s a good project.

Looking at it this way, I am indeed taking action. So what makes me feel such extreme emptiness? I look back. Before writing this record, I looked at Xiaohongshu, scrolled through QQ, and browsed channels. What was I doing? Looking for friends. When I got home, I logged into my old QQ and saw that I actually made quite a few attempts: I added several group chats, but I dare not post anything (it’s so funny). I added over a dozen online friends, but most conversations died after a few lines. I matched with several people on a dating platform, but they all went silent after chatting for a few lines that day. Speaking of which, perhaps the most successful social interaction was the club. I could recognize each other with several people. This is my most successful social interaction, oh my god. And this is based on my result of going to activities for training every week, not on actual social interaction. Some people become close after just a few times, but it took me two semesters to be able to call out names and match numbers. Really…

There have been many failures in offline socializing, and many online ones too, but it’s generally easier than offline. However, when it comes to making friends, what can I talk about? If it’s just randomly adding a friend. Even if I matched with someone on a dating platform, and I was prepared just to add a friend and expand my contacts, every time we chatted for a few lines, it would fall into long silence. I still feel helpless. I don’t know what to talk about, sharing life? Will it be weird; sharing a math problem? That’s too mysterious… Moreover, speaking of which, I already have online friends with whom I can share life, and high school classmates with whom I can share math problems, but I still feel a lack. What do I really need? I don’t know, maybe it’s a closer relationship, a soul connection? I don’t know, but maybe it is.

Speaking of which, I hardly felt empty this semester, but I felt empty again when I got home. I thought that I had filled my days so completely. During the day, I studied while distracted, and at night, I happily played games. I practiced concentrating before bed, and I exercised in the morning. This didn’t leave me with much time for confusion. But objectively, I am still confused, and I still lack some in-depth communication. When I get home, with a lot of free time, it all explodes.

I also find it difficult to avoid emptiness with similar means; it’s different at home. At school, I am surrounded by students; at home, I almost only have myself. My family has almost no gravity on me; I feel a huge generation gap. I seem to exist on two sides of the same world as my parents, and on two sides of the world with my sister, who is 10 years younger. She babbles all day, which is a normal childlike nature, but what she babbles about are bad internet memes… It’s hard for us to have effective in-depth communication; there are no common topics. All my interests and hobbies are orthogonal to theirs. Or rather, I hardly know what their interests are; life seems to fill them up completely. Besides labor necessary for survival, it’s scrolling through phones, watching dramas, and looking forward to being wealthy. I don’t intend to criticize; I am just lost, feeling atomized. University provided all the conditions for atomization. I didn’t intentionally choose it, but I have fallen into a state of atomization unconsciously, struggling in confusion. I try to break free, trying to socialize, but I am at a loss, while people already have ten thousand friends.

Maybe I should have gone to another school, to continue a life familiar in collectivism? I don’t know, but it’s already impossible. Maybe I will just be lost alone in group activities, who knows. It’s not that I haven’t participated in group activities; as long as the number of people present is >=3, I can effectively become invisible, which is a surprisingly powerful passive skill.

In short, in short, I have made progress. I can greet others naturally, and I can do a pre-presentation without being timid… Although it seems like a very basic skill, it’s something I gained after many attempts. Oh, speaking of which, I really am like a problem child, but I’m not a child anymore.

I hope the gloomy tone doesn’t let your mood be covered in dust. If you are willing, I would also be happy to hear your thoughts.
