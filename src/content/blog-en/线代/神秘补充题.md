# Two
Let $ A, B $ be a complex matrix of order $ m, n $, with eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_m $ and $\mu_1, \dots, \mu_n $. Define the map $\mathcal{S}$ as a transformation on the matrix space $ M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})$:

$$
\mathcal{S} : X \mapsto AX - XB + AXB^2
$$

1) Linear Transformation Proof Proof $\mathcal{S}$ is a linear transformation on a matrix space. $ M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})$ 2) Find the eigenvalues and the transformation $\mathcal{S}$ of all eigenvalues, and express it using $ A $ and $ B $ the eigenvalues of. $ A, B $ If it can be diagonalized,$\alpha_i $ is $ A $ belongs to the eigenvalue $\lambda_i $ of (right) eigenvectors.$\beta_j $ is $ B^T $ belongs to the eigenvalue $\mu_j $ the (right) eigenvectors. Prove $\mathcal{S}$ is diagonalizable on $ M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})$ the. Write down $\mathcal{S}$ the eigenvectors belonging to each eigenvalue.

## 1) Linear Transformation Proof

**Proof that $\mathcal{S}$ is a linear transformation:**

For any $ X, Y \in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})$ and any $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{C}$, we need to verify:

$$
\mathcal{S}(\alpha X + \beta Y) = \alpha \mathcal{S}(X) + \beta \mathcal{S}(Y)
$$

Calculate the left side:

$$
\mathcal{S}(\alpha X + \beta Y) = A(\alpha X + \beta Y) - (\alpha X + \beta Y)B + A(\alpha X + \beta Y)B^2
$$

Expand:

$$
= \alpha AX + \beta AY - \alpha XB - \beta YB + \alpha AXB^2 + \beta AYB^2
$$

$$
= \alpha(AX - XB + AXB^2) + \beta(AY - YB + AYB^2)
$$

$$
= \alpha \mathcal{S}(X) + \beta \mathcal{S}(Y)
$$

**Therefore, $\mathcal{S}$ is a linear transformation.** ∎

## 2) Eigenvalue Solving

**Key Observation:** Rewrite $\mathcal{S}$ as:

$$
\mathcal{S}(X) = AX(I + BB) - XB = AX(I + B^2) - XB
$$

Write more clearly as:

$$
\mathcal{S}(X) = A X (I_n + B^2) - X B
$$

This is similar to the form of the **Sylvester transformation**. Vectorize using the **Kronecker product**:

$$
\text{vec}(\mathcal{S}(X)) = \left[(I_n + B^2)^T \otimes A - B^T \otimes I_m\right] \text{vec}(X)
$$

**Calculate the eigenvalues of $\mathcal{S}$:**

Let $\lambda_i $ be an eigenvalue of $ A $, and $\mu_j $ be an eigenvalue of $ B $ (and thus also an eigenvalue of $ B^T $).

For the Kronecker product, the following property holds:
- The eigenvalues of $(I_n + B^2)^T = I_n + (B^T)^2 $ are $ 1 + \mu_j^2 $ (corresponding eigenvectors are the same as those for $ B^T $)

Therefore, the eigenvalues of the transformation matrix are:

$$
\boxed{\nu_{ij} = (1 + \mu_j^2)\lambda_i - \mu_j = \lambda_i + \lambda_i \mu_j^2 - \mu_j}
$$

Where $ i = 1, \ldots, m $, $ j = 1, \ldots, n $.

**All eigenvalues of $\mathcal{S}$ are:**

$$
\nu_{ij} = \lambda_i(1 + \mu_j^2) - \mu_j, \quad i=1,\ldots,m,\; j=1,\ldots,n
$$

## 3) Diagonalization and Eigenvectors

**Proof that $\mathcal{S}$ is diagonalizable:**

Since $ A $ and $ B $ are both diagonalizable, then:
- $ A = P\Lambda P^{-1}$, where $\Lambda = \text{diag}(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_m)$-$ B = QMQ^{-1}$, where $ M = \text{diag}(\mu_1, \ldots, \mu_n)$**construct the eigenvectors of $\mathcal{S}$:**

Let $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{C}^m $ be a right eigenvector of $ A $ corresponding to $\lambda_i $: $ A\alpha_i = \lambda_i \alpha_i $. Let $\beta_j \in \mathbb{C}^n $ be a right eigenvector of $ B^T $ corresponding to $\mu_j $: $ B^T\beta_j = \mu_j \beta_j $, i.e., $\beta_j^T B = \mu_j \beta_j^T $**define the matrix** $ E_{ij} = \alpha_i \beta_j^T \in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})$**verify that $ E_{ij}$ is an eigenvector of $\mathcal{S}$:**

Calculate $\mathcal{S}(E_{ij})$:

$$
\mathcal{S}(\alpha_i \beta_j^T) = A(\alpha_i \beta_j^T) - (\alpha_i \beta_j^T)B + A(\alpha_i \beta_j^T)B^2
$$

Calculate term by term:
- $ A(\alpha_i \beta_j^T) = (A\alpha_i)\beta_j^T = \lambda_i \alpha_i \beta_j^T $-$(\alpha_i \beta_j^T)B = \alpha_i (\beta_j^T B) = \alpha_i (\mu_j \beta_j^T) = \mu_j \alpha_i \beta_j^T $-$ A(\alpha_i \beta_j^T)B^2 = (A\alpha_i)(\beta_j^T B^2) = \lambda_i \alpha_i \cdot \mu_j^2 \beta_j^T = \lambda_i \mu_j^2 \alpha_i \beta_j^T $

Therefore:

$$
\mathcal{S}(E_{ij}) = \lambda_i E_{ij} - \mu_j E_{ij} + \lambda_i \mu_j^2 E_{ij} = (\lambda_i - \mu_j + \lambda_i \mu_j^2)E_{ij}
$$

$$
\boxed{\mathcal{S}(E_{ij}) = \left[\lambda_i(1 + \mu_j^2) - \mu_j\right] E_{ij}}
$$

**Conclusion:**
- **$ E_{ij} = \alpha_i \beta_j^T $ is an eigenvector of $\mathcal{S}$ corresponding to the eigenvalue $\nu_{ij} = \lambda_i(1+\mu_j^2) - \mu_j $**
- Since $ A, B $ has $ m, n $ linearly independent eigenvectors, we obtain $ mn $ matrices $ E_{ij}$, which form a basis for $ M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})$ (because $\{\alpha_i\}$ and $\{\beta_j\}$ are bases)
- **Therefore, $\mathcal{S}$ is diagonalizable** ∎
