# A Clear Fragrance in the Midst of Turmoil

Fat white ducks float on the bean-green river. Outside, the guns can blaze and the earth can shake. The Dragon Boat Festival is still the Dragon Boat Festival, and the firecrackers still give their long cry. There is a loveliness in people who trust one another without a second thought. There is love struck like a spark from flint. Here all that is beautiful in the world seems to gather, and the eye lingers. Yet a flower in full bloom will wither, and a clear river will often turn muddy in the end. Fate plays with people, and tragedy comes on the heels of beauty. The elder brother follows Ophelia. The younger is set on leaving it all far behind. Stone and brick cover Grandfather's body, and only Cuicui is left on the bow, looking into the distance. The border town may still be lively, I suppose. It is a different generation's bustle now.

A song in praise of peace, a sound that carries quiet. The author put his feelings there. We had worked all this out in class, and anything more I say would be picking over the scraps. This beauty comes to grief in the end, and that is part of how it becomes beautiful. From birth, every living thing is on its way toward death, and death too makes it what it is. When you see people scattered and driven from home, how can you help remembering the joy of coming together? When the guns thunder, how can you help remembering the quiet of peace? Even now, with all quiet and nothing amiss, I find myself longing for what is so unworldly and so fine in *Border Town*.

Fine rice comes out of the rough grain. Fine silk comes out of coarse thread. What seems beyond the dust of this world comes from the dust itself, and finds a nimbler way to the quick of life. Who has no fields of fancy in their heart? And how many people can rest easy in those fields? The present world holds the body fast. Things from outside keep tugging at the soul. And so the border town in the heart grows unsteady and far away. How to scoop up a handful of clear water from all that mud, how to catch a trace of clean fragrance in the turmoil, becomes something one stands on tiptoe to reach. A thousand people have a thousand answers, just as they have a thousand Hamlets. I'll venture a word of my own: may you gentlemen climb to a height and write a rhapsody there.

The world flashes with strange and dazzling things. An unspoiled heart is enough to see through their spell. If you know clearly where your own heart draws you, you won't lose your bearings in a gale. Money and fame: I wouldn't pronounce either of them corrupt, though how many heads have they turned? In a narrow channel a drop of water can run with the current and never wonder whether it's going the wrong way. Once it reaches the shoreless sea, where does it go? It may do nothing but sink among currents all pulling different ways. Why not stop a moment? Take that idle, tedious time with nothing to do, time that is purely your own, and let your thoughts wander. Touch the one drop of water left on a green leaf. Listen quietly to birds calling among the branches. Perhaps they can lead you to a border town that belongs to you alone. Somewhere, in a corner of this jumbled world, such a town opens its gates to you. You can wash off what remains of the haze and see what is truly yourself.

The border town in the heart lets a person be at ease with themselves. A solid quiet gives one's feelings somewhere to settle. You might slip free of the mire like a cicada from its shell, taking none of the world's dirt with you. You might stir up the mud and raise the waves, eat the wine dregs and sip the thin liquor. If you follow what is true in your heart, I suppose you can find some little place to be at peace.

I have ventured to set down what I feel, and offer this short preface with respect. Now let each of you pour forth a river like Pan Yue's, a sea like Lu Ji's.
