I wrote this after reading a bit of Xu Zhimo (

* * *

The fierce sun blazes.  
The gold of its light is holy.  
It seems about to wash the darkness out of the world.

In that gold,  
even the lovely red flower takes on the breath of the Holy Spirit.  
A petal trembles,  
comes loose—

In that instant  
time seems suddenly to set.

At the boundary of life and death,  
the petal falling, still to fall,  
holds that instant down.

Space, light and shadow, existence—  
the scene  
might be a painting that will never change.

She takes a little step, light as a lotus,  
and enters the picture.

And the picture  
has one more color—

**Beauty beyond compare.**

* * *

The clear moon shines in the silver pool.  
A faint smile, like a flower,  
two eyes like stars.

Moonlight pours into the pool.  
A breeze is startled awake.  
The surface rises in little ripples.

The moon in the water  
sways—  
sways—

Its drifting image  
finally grows still.

A sprig of green willow  
reaches into the moon.

The moon, so far from common things,  
now teems with life  
and takes on the breath of this world.

If you wonder whether a willow branch can be in the moon,  
you have only to lift your eyes and look straight ahead—

That pair of moons  
is still clear and light in her.

The stars crowd in beside them.  
She catches me looking  
and smiles a little—

The light in those eyes outshines the stars.

* * *

Cold rain comes down,  
scattering drops before the flowers.

Rain, close upon close,  
weaves itself into a strange net—  
holding the flowers,  
covering the flowers,  
watering the flowers.

The flower bows its neck a little.  
With the clean scent of the soil  
its own rises clearer.

It is—  
pure and plain,  
holy and ordinary.

It can take in a hundred scents and stay itself,  
and go all the deeper into you.

And yet,  
when a breath of something fainter by far appears—

The smell of flowers and of earth  
falls away into the background.

It is—  
a breath I cannot quite name,  
coming from—

**Her.**

It is she  
whom words learned in this world,  
fit for this world,  
can scarcely describe—

**Her.**

It is she  
who has set another treasure  
in the innermost chamber of my soul—

**Her.**

You cannot say what it is.  
You are left bewildered.  
You are left rapt.

* * *

She—  
that cloud drifting in and out of sight;

She—  
that silver moon in the far-off dark;

She—  
the flower of beauty that took root in the heart.

It is hard to imagine  
that this can be only a fragment of a soul.

Already it has  
the power to shake the heart like this.

You cannot help wondering:  
if that soul ever came into its fullness,  
what delight there would be,  
what joy.

It would be—  
fuller,  
clearer, lighter.

And by then,  
what would remain in the foreground?  
What could do anything but set it off?

Everything  
would be there only to set it off.

There would be a new world.

A world  
wholly imagined,  
beautiful.

A land of immortals  
could hardly surpass it.

When they come,  
one after the next,  
one, and then another—

They might make a world:

> A world you could  
> really touch.

**Soul.**

That is another realm.

Far off though it is,  
though it stays beyond reach for now,  
it can still set you on fire.

* * *

I gaze, lost—

At her in the moonlight.
