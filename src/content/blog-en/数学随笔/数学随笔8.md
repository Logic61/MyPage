## A real symmetric matrix can be orthogonally diagonalized
### 1. Theorem Description 
A real symmetric matrix $ A $ must be writable as $ A = PDP^{-1} = PDP^T $, where $ P $ is an orthogonal matrix, and $ D $ is a real diagonal matrix. 
### 2. Proof Logic using Mathematical Induction 
**Assumption**:$ n-1 $ Any real symmetric matrix can be orthogonally diagonalized. **Consider $ n $ real symmetric matrix $ A $:** 
1. **Constructing a Basis**: Let $\lambda_1 $ be a real eigenvalue of $ A $, and $\alpha_1 $ be its corresponding unit eigenvector. Extend $\alpha_1 $ to a basis of $\mathbb{R}^n $, and after orthogonalization and normalization, obtain the standard orthonormal basis $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n $, which forms the orthogonal matrix $ P = [\alpha_1 \quad \alpha_2 \dots \alpha_n]$. 
2. **Matrix Transformation**:

$$
A [\alpha_1 \quad \alpha_2 \dots \alpha_n] = [\lambda_1 \alpha_1 \quad A\alpha_2 \dots A\alpha_n]
$$

Transform to block matrix form:

$$
P^T A P = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & B \end{bmatrix}
$$

(Since $ P^T A P $ is still a symmetric matrix, the other elements in the first row must be $ 0 $)
3. **Applying the Inductive Hypothesis**: For a real symmetric matrix of order $ n-1 $, $ B $, there exists an orthogonal matrix $ P_1 $ such that $ P_1^T B P_1 = D_1 $. 4. **Final Diagonalization**: 

$$
A = P \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & P_1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & D_1 \end{bmatrix} \left( P \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & P_1 \end{bmatrix} \right)^T
$$

## Schur Decomposition of Complex Matrices

### 1. Theorem Description
**Theorem**: Let $ A $ be a complex square matrix of order $ n $, then there must exist a **Unitary Matrix** $ U $ (satisfying $ U^H U = I $), such that:

$$
U^H A U = T
$$

where $ T $ is an **upper triangular matrix**, and its diagonal elements are the eigenvalues of $ A $.

---

### 2. Proof Process using Mathematical Induction
The logic of this proof is very similar to the orthogonal diagonalization of real symmetric matrices, but it is performed in the complex domain.

**A. Base Step ($ n=1 $)**
The matrix $ 1 \times 1 $ itself is an upper triangular matrix, so the conclusion is obviously true.

**B. Inductive Hypothesis**
Assume that all complex matrices of order $ n-1 $ can be transformed into an upper triangular matrix via a unitary transformation.

**C. Inductive Step ($ n-1 \to n $)**
1. **Finding an Eigenpair**: Since we are in the complex domain $\mathbb{C}$, by the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, $ A $ must have at least one eigenvalue $\lambda_1 $ and its corresponding unit eigenvector $ u_1 $.
2. **Constructing a Unitary Matrix**: Extend $ u_1 $ to a standard orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{C}^n $, and construct the unitary matrix $ U_1 = [u_1, u_2, \dots, u_n]$.
3. **Block Transformation**:

$$
U_1^H A U_1 = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & \mathbf{w}^H \\ \mathbf{0} & A_{n-1} \end{bmatrix}
$$

Here, $ A_{n-1}$ is a complex matrix of order $ n-1 $. *Note: Since $ A $ is not necessarily a symmetric/Hermitian matrix, the top-right corner $\mathbf{w}^H $ is usually non-zero.*
4. **Nested Induction**: By assumption, there exists a unitary matrix $ V $ of order $ n-1 $, such that $ V^H A_{n-1} V = T_{n-1}$ is an upper triangular matrix.
5. **Final Composition**: Let $ U = U_1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & V \end{bmatrix}$, then $ U^H A U $ is an upper triangular matrix.
## Spectral Theorem for Normal Matrices
**Proposition**:$ A $ is a normal matrix (i.e. $ AA^H = A^HA $) is a necessary and sufficient condition for $ A $ to be unitarily similar to a diagonal matrix.

> [!abstract] Inductive Logic
> * **Base Case**: $ n=1 $ is obviously true.
> * **Inductive Step**: 
>   1. Take an eigenvalue and its unit eigenvector $ u_1 $ of $ A $.
>   2. Construct the unitary matrix $ U = [u_1, u_2, \dots, u_n]$.
>   3. Calculate $ U^H A U = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & \mathbf{w}^H \\ \mathbf{0} & A_{n-1} \end{bmatrix}$.
>   4. **Key Point**: Utilize the fact that $ A $ is a normal matrix to prove $\mathbf{w}^H = \mathbf{0}$, and that $ A_{n-1}$ is still a normal matrix.
>   5. Apply the inductive hypothesis to $ A_{n-1}$.
