Count it twice.

If we choose $ k $ representatives from $ n $ people, we either choose $ X $ or we don't.
These cases give $\binom{n-1}{k-1}$ and $\binom{n-1}{k}$, respectively, so

$$
\dbinom{n-1}{k-1}+\dbinom{n-1}{k}=\dbinom{n}{k}
$$

Another identity:

$$
\sum_{r=0}^n\dbinom{n}{r}\dbinom{2n}{n-r}=\dbinom{3n}{n}
$$

> **Proof.**
> Let $ S $ be the set of ways to choose $ n $ balls from $ n $ red balls and $ 2n $ green balls.
> $|S|=\dbinom{3n}{n}$, since we are simply choosing $ n $ balls from a total of $ 3n $.
>
> Now consider choosing $ r $ red balls. We must also choose $ n-r $ green balls, giving
> $\dbinom{n}{r}\dbinom{2n}{n-r}$ possibilities. Summing over all cases gives $|S|$.
>
> These two ways of counting the same set prove the identity.
