Proofs of Cauchy inequality types seem to be only for specific problems, but they are truly ingenious.
They mainly utilize non-negativity combined with the discriminant of a quadratic function to complete.



### Classic Cauchy Inequality

$$
(\sum a_ib_i)^2 \le (\sum a_i^2)(\sum b_i^2)
$$

We construct:

$$
f(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n (a_it + b_i)^2 \ge 0
$$

$$
f(x) = (\sum a_i^2)t^2 + 2(\sum a_ib_i)t + (\sum b_i^2) \ge 0
$$

The discriminant with respect to t being zero gives:

$$
\Delta = [2(\sum a_ib_i)]^2 - 4(\sum a_i^2)(\sum b_i^2) \le 0
$$

After rearranging, we get $(\sum a_ib_i)^2 \le (\sum a_i^2)(\sum b_i^2)$.
### Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality

$$
(\int_a^b f(x)g(x)dx)^2 \le \int_a^bf(x)^2dx \cdot \int_a^b g(x)^2dx
$$

Construct the following non-negative integral:

$$
\int_a^b (f(x)+tg(x))^2dx=\int_a^b f(x)^2dx+\int_a^b 2tf(x)g(x)dx+\int_a^b t^2g(x)^2dx\ge 0
$$

Thus, we know that the discriminant with respect to t is less than zero:

$$
(\int_a^b f(x)g(x)dx)^2 \le \int_a^bf(x)^2dx \cdot \int_a^b g(x)^2dx
$$

This is also the Cauchy integral inequality.
Small application: If we set $ g(x)=\frac{1}{f(x)}$, then:

$$
\int_a^b \frac{1}{f(x)} dx \ge \frac{(b-a)^2}{\int_a^b f(x)dx}
$$

### Triangle Inequality

$$
||u+v|| \le ||u||+||v||
$$

We still construct:

$$
(u+tv,u+tv) \ge 0
$$

Expanding gives:

$$
(u,u)+2t(u,v)+t^2(v,v) \ge0
$$

Using the discriminant being less than 0:

$$
(u,v)^2 \le (u,u)(v,v)
$$

This is actually the so-called Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
