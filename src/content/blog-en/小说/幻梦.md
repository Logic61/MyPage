Who can tell the real from the unreal?  
Dream and waking flow into one another.  
Who says that in the dream-country of Huaxu  
nothing true can remain?


There were bound to be hard times in a life, he supposed. With that in mind, he told his uncle plainly how things stood. The novel he'd spent a year on had found no one who cared for it and no one who would publish it. He'd finally used up what his parents had left him, which hadn't been much, and now he couldn't pay the rent. His uncle was rich, and had always been generous. They hadn't seen each other in the years he'd been living away, but perhaps his uncle might lend him a hand. Sure enough, his uncle heard his nephew out patiently after all those years apart. Then something flashed in his eyes, a look of calculation, almost shrewd. But rich men, however generous, were bound to be shrewd, he supposed. It meant very little. See, the look was gone in a moment, and his uncle's face relaxed into a kindly smile.

“Xiao Yu, I've an estate with a room or two empty. You can stay there for now.”

“A manor?”

“Yes. There are a few people there already, but there's room left. No need to stand on ceremony. We're family. The place is out of the way, and I'm happy to have as many tenants as I've got. If you don't mind the company, make yourself at home.”

“Really?” 

“Why be so formal with your uncle.”

“I feel too embarrassed!” 

“What’s the matter!” 

…

He was taken aback by the favor, but one thought of the sorry state he was in, and he accepted it, shyly, all the same. The estate really was remote. The car drove for a long time, past tree after tree, and finally stopped before an iron gate. Behind it stretched a great flat lawn, with a Western-style villa lying idle on the grass. He had a moment to reflect on the distance between rich and poor, then stepped through the gate. The man who'd come with him snapped it shut. For no good reason he thought of a cage, looking at the fence that went on and on. He remembered, too, how horror stories from the West seemed especially fond of places like this. But the sky over the estate was full of stars, and there was only the one house on all that grass.

He didn't realize he had arrived at the villa's entrance. There were three people inside; perhaps his uncle had already greeted them, and the three were on the sofa, seemingly waiting for him. As he stepped into the house, they all stood up. A child ran straight over,

“Number 4!”

The child called out, pointing at him, looking at a tall man, then lowered his hand and called out,

“Number 3!”

Then he said no more. The man strolled over and said to him:

“Number 1, research theoretical physics.”

“Hello.”

Although confused, he could only greet them.

“You two have confused him. New friend, may I ask what your name is?” 

“Yu, the feather in ‘feather’.”

He then met the eyes of a woman; she appeared to be in her prime, with a beautiful face that gave off a gentle and graceful feeling. Strangely, there was a certain light emanating from her eyes, a light that even a man not good at reading people could catch. It seemed like curiosity, or perhaps, liking? He quickly dismissed this thought as delusion due to his long period of being single. After all, no one falls in love at first sight; he didn't believe it.
 “Mr. Mo Yu, you can call me Number 2; I am a composer. You might find our names strange; they were suggested by the manor owner, Mr. Mo.” 

“They require us to use the full numbers 1, 2, 3, and 4?” 

“It is your choice; Number 4 can use your original name.” 

“Must you?” 

“Must.”

“Why? Does he have that right?” 

“He agreed to reduce the rent.”

At this time, Number 1 and Number 3 left without a word.

“These two are not talkative; please forgive us.”

He felt a little strange.

“It's fine; I'm not talkative either.”

“Hehe, is that so. Mr. Mo, um, are you perhaps also Mr. Mo?”

“Yes.”

“The landlord has prepaid your meal expenses for me, and I will prepare your ingredients. Will you cook for yourself, or shall I take care of it?”

“Is it like that? Then I'll trouble you, Miss.”

“Okay. You can just call me Number 2.”

“Number 2, I feel something is strange. You can just call me by my name or say ‘you’; no need for titles.”

“No problem, Mr. Mo.”

“This, I originally thought composers were always high and proud, perhaps like—Number 1.”

“Not at all.”

She finally moved her starlike eyes, turned her back to him, and looked out the window,

“Art comes from life, you know. I often get inspiration from trivial matters, and I have even worked as a host. Observing guests leisurely, capturing inspiration in work, is not a bad thing. Of course, this is thanks to the fact that the ecology is not flourishing.”

She turned around and smiled sweetly,

“I'll take you to your room.”

“The second floor is where Number 1, Number 3, and I live. Behind that light green door is my room. The red door is Number 3's room. The blue door is Number 1's room.”

“Does the child sleep by himself?”

“Please don't talk about that child.”

Her expression suddenly became solemn, paused at this point, and then suddenly relaxed,

“The child is not small.”

He found it strange, but finally did not say anything.

“The third floor belongs to you. This staircase requires facial recognition to enter.”

“Ah? A whole floor, and why does it require facial recognition?”

“I don't know; I apologize. You can go up by yourself.”

Outside the staircase, which resembled a utility room, was a facial recognition device.

“If you need anything, please feel free to knock on my door.”

He nodded to her and went upstairs alone.

The third floor was not large. He recalled the villa he saw before; the top floor was only one or two levels below $\frac{1}{4}$, so it seemed to be designed for one person to live in. The furnishings were simple, and there was a piano in the corner, and everything was very complete. Outside the floor-to-ceiling window was a terrace, very spacious. At this moment, his uncle called him on the phone:

“Xiao Yu, Number 2 introduced you to the house, right? How is it?”

“I feel deeply ashamed to receive such kindness. It's very good. But... may I ask why you make the tenants use numbers?”

“Well, it's actually very simple, but it's not something you can understand for now.”

“It's not something I can understand?”

“Perhaps you will know soon. For now, wouldn't keeping some mystery make life more interesting?”

“So, why do I need facial recognition to go up to the third floor?”

“Wouldn't that give you some sense of security? An unfamiliar tenant, who knows if they don't have some bad intentions?”

“Uncle, you've gone to trouble.”

…

He walked onto the rooftop and looked down. It was unlikely any thief could get up here, and with the hallway, it did indeed feel somewhat secure.

Come to think of it, Number 2 didn't seem to have properly introduced the interior layout, so he decided to walk around himself. Going down to the second floor, all the doors were closed, and the lights were on in every room. It seems the tenants are all homebodies. A composer, a physicist, no wonder they are willing to live here. Do people like that have bad intentions? The third floor only has one bathroom, but the second floor has two, plus a bathroom. The first floor, besides a spacious hall and a kitchen, also has an upright pipe organ in the corner of the hall, which he hadn't noticed before. It was the first time Mo Yu had seen it, and out of curiosity, he sat on the organ bench, just about to play, when he was suddenly constrained by a sense of morality. But he thought, if it belonged to Uncle, he could naturally play it; if it belonged to Number 2, there was a possibility, but Number 2 wasn't the type to care about such things. But if it belonged to Number 1, recalling the cold face from their first meeting, Mo Yu hesitated a little, but this very small possibility was finally overcome by curiosity. He didn't even think that it might disturb others, and impatiently played Bach's The Well-Tempered Clavier. When the piece ended, while he was still savoring the unique feel and tone, he suddenly heard applause erupt from behind. It was Number 2, who had come down to the first floor without knowing, and stopped behind Mo Yu.

“Perhaps I played this when I was 8, or 9.”

Number 2 blinked and said to Mo Yu.

“Is that so? Then perhaps this will bring back your memories.”

“It will.”

“This piano, is it?”

“It belongs to the landlord. You can play it freely.”

“Will Number 1 and Number 3 mind?”
 
“I have agreed with them that you can borrow it anytime between lunch and dinner.”

“Very accommodating.”

“Yes, I should say that's also why I could live here.”

“Do you compose for the pipe organ?”

“I originally composed mostly for the piano. A few days ago, I planned to go somewhere quiet to find inspiration, and I happened to see this place, so I felt like writing Baroque pieces, so I also composed for the pipe organ... But it can be played on the piano too.”

“Can I listen to? Your work.”

Yi Wan'er:

“I'm showing off.”

Yi sat on the organ bench, sitting in the spot Mo Yu had just occupied. Yi carefully moved the piano stool and sat down. Crossing her ten fingers and clasping them, she let out a breath after a few moments. She placed her hands on the keys. As her hands fell, a melody began to appear, flowing through one voice after another... Even Mo Yu, as an amateur, could only discern these. The piece ended. He thought for a moment, thinking that shouting Bravo was strange, and remembering Yi's applause earlier, he also clapped his hands softly a few times. Number 2 looked at him and smirked:

“How was it?”

“You know, I'm an amateur.”

“Is writing different from music, in that music is an art?”

“Then I can only make up a few lines.”

“No need to be polite. Mr. Mo. I sincerely hope to hear how a writer views my music. You just need to talk about your direct feelings.”

Although he listened attentively, he didn't really know what to say. In his mind at that moment, instead, vivid images of fresh fingers and cascades of black hair appeared... Although he was indeed listening, recalling the scene, it was fortunate that he could remember the theme. Number 2 still looked at him, showing complete patience, so he calmed his messy thoughts, trying to find those “direct feelings” as Number 2 suggested. It was probably a tone leaning towards joy rather than sorrow, further...

“Can I try to use a metaphor?”

“Of course.”

“A child entering a forest for the first time, looking around with delight, dense leaves, beautiful small flowers—”

“This is what I wrote when I first arrived.”

“Then perhaps my feelings are not biased?”

“The interpreter is free.”

“Is that so?”

“I thought so. However, isn't the intention of the performer to convey through sound?”

“Are you saying that if the performer says there is a deer, the listener shouldn't hear it as a horse?”

“Unless the performer's skill is limited, or their articulation is poor.”

“Perhaps there are certain requirements for the listener as well?”
 
“Yes, otherwise, why would there be the story of Bo Ya and the broken string?”

Yi paused,

“You listen very accurately. We've been going in circles just now.”

“Perhaps not a big circle.”

“Are you saying it's okay to chat with me about this?”

“Exploring relative unfamiliar fields should be very inspiring. Music should be the same, right?”

“Very much so! Then, when you're free, could you talk to me about writing articles too?”

“With great pleasure.”

“It's getting late. I'm going to cook.”

“Thank you for your trouble, Miss.”

“It's nothing.”

His gaze lingered on her back, her shoulder-length hair, her fair arms, her slender legs, her graceful steps, reminding him of her sweet scent, her fairy-like voice, the fugue she played that she wrote herself, her radiant eyes—everything, a source of ethereal beauty. All of this wove a net, enclosing him. He suddenly thought that the next moment he would wake up in a dilapidated rented room. He discovered that the few hours were merely a phantom dream. As her figure disappeared from sight, the beautiful afterimage still displayed the smile of spring in his mind. Wasn't he exchanging a light yet profoundly weighty promise with such an ethereal and graceful woman in a flash of time? Wasn't her invitation to talk an openness to deepening the connection? But thinking of her gentle nature, he felt a little doubtful that it was merely polite words, like the sun's gentle shine or a light breeze.

In his wandering thoughts like light smoke, he faintly heard footsteps, and Number 2 appeared around the corner.

“I forgot to ask, Mr. Mo, what would you like to eat?”

“I don't have any preference for food; anything is fine.”

“You saying that makes me a little conflicted.”

“Just make an extra portion of what you usually make; no need to trouble yourself.”

“Is that so? That's fine too.”

After a while, Number 2 brought out a steaming bowl of noodles.

“Why bother Miss to bring it out? Miss just call out, and I'll go serve it.”

“It's a small thing. I said it, and I've done it before; it's also a form of reminiscence for me.”

“But…”

“Also, — please don't call me Miss anymore; it just feels wrong.”

“When you call me 'Sir,' don't you feel something is wrong?”

“Perhaps a little, but calling me by name seems inappropriate.”

“Inappropriate? How about calling me by my given name?”

“That, is acceptable.”

“Wing letter. Bird covers its wings, the letter conceals its tracks.”

“Concealment by letter, do you mean the character for letter?”

“Yes.”

“Wing letter?”

“Eh! In that case, perhaps may I ask your name?”

“But…”

“Tell me, how did Uncle know if we corresponded to 1, 2, and 3?”

“Perhaps he was just joking and lowering the price?”

“Then why not set a low price from the beginning?”

“Well, I don't know. Actually, I didn't care about those things. Agreeing was just a whim. If you want to know…”

She lowered her head slightly, as if pondering something. After a while,

“Gan Tang.”

“Gan Tang.”

“Yes. Um—Wing Letter, eat quickly. The noodles are getting cold.”

Mo Yu nodded, turning his attention to the noodles.

Gan Tang did not eat with him. When he asked, she only said she had already eaten, so he did not ask further. After that, he went upstairs to write and sleep. The next day, Gan Tang had already prepared breakfast, and she had also eaten. In fact, over the following period, he always ate alone and never saw the three tenants eating. But this was probably not that important? After waiting a while, he planned to invite Gan Tang to eat at the same table to discuss art and things like that. In the waiting and the waiting, he finally turned this into an unspoken doubt. As he became closer, as he became more familiar, the doubt finally turned into a hope. It seemed it should be so. Just waiting for him to discover or ask for a change.

You would think it easy to grow close to people living in such cramped quarters. But in a whole week Mo Yu saw No. 1 and No. 3 so seldom he could count the encounters on his fingers, despite No. 3 being a child. The two moved together like shadows, and something about them always unsettled him. When they crossed paths, No. 1 would nod and go on. No. 3 said nothing. Those eyes, for some reason so empty, would fix on Mo Yu until he found himself unable to speak. So it was almost a world for two, for him and Gan Tang. She read what he wrote, he listened to what she composed, and they gave each other an outsider's advice. Time flowed across the weave of his manuscript paper, through the notes that sprang from the harpsichord. All at once, a fortnight was gone.

When he recalled this period, if he had to say that something opened Pandora's box, it might be this day. When Gan Tang suddenly asked him to listen to her most proud piece for the piano, but sighed that the harmonium lacked expressiveness. He told Gan Tang that there was a piano on the third floor.

“Eh? Really? Why did you only say it now?”

“Uh, I just feel…”

“Sorry, I know. But, I always feel like you have the air of a gentle lady, hehe.”

“What are you talking about. Self-restraint is necessary for longer development. What begins with madness must end with madness.”

“Then, do you have to restrain yourself today?”

“No, let's go.”

Gan Tang smiled and followed Mo Yu upstairs.

“After so long, can you not play?”

“I usually play at night, but I'm afraid of disturbing you, so I play softly…”

“Then, I'll start playing.”

She still held both hands together, gazing at the keys for a moment, and slowly let out a breath, placing her ten fingers on the black and white keys. Flowing between grandeur and elegance, suppression and outburst, winding and direct, it was clearly a beautiful piece of music.

“How was it?”

After the lingering notes faded, she turned her head and smiled, asking.

“Very moving. But in my memory, there was only a relatively simple moving feeling. If I were to comment better, perhaps, I would need to listen a few more times.”

“Is listening every day enough?”

“It is enough.”

“Then, is this novel you are currently writing on this table?”

“Yes.”

“Keep it up, I look forward to its completion. By the way, “Feathers of the Flowing Moon” that you showed me before was very interesting.”

“Is it? The one I read first. That was written when I was in junior high school. You probably saw that later, the plot was a bit broken.”

“It is a bit, like you didn't make an outline? Although it was wrapped up, some parts of the plot seemed indeed abrupt, like the rebirth of the world, if written in, the volume might double.”

“Yes. But I didn't care about that at the time, I just wanted to convey a mood, so I was simply absurd.”

“Composers sometimes also temporarily break the rules to pursue something. Alright, it's getting late, I should go cook.”

A normal conversation, but it happened in Mo Yu's bedroom. The person was gone, but a faint, sweet scent could still be smelled, and the sound of the piano and laughter still echoed in his ears. Even when he lay in bed later, he vaguely felt a delicate fragrance. He couldn't help but look back at that divine smile.

> “Is listening every day enough?”

He still remembered these words clearly, which spread out a small garden path before him. In the coming days, he would walk freely on this path full of flowers, fragrance, and spring light. The breath of life would fill his heart, and this would probably also inspire his writing passion. The future would take the form of Gan Tang, extending a jade arm in a faint smile, inviting him to dance together.

The heat rises, and it is about to meet the cold, and finally condenses into cold rain.

The next day. Just as he was about to lead Gan Tang upstairs, he suddenly heard footsteps stop behind him. He turned around and saw her looking sideways at the open red door, and then suddenly turned to look at him. He was too slow to react, and as he lost his balance, he heard a sound similar to metal striking—*clang*.

It deflated. His head deflated. He saw it, Gan Tang's head deflated. However, not a drop of blood flowed out; it only fell from her head, as she got up, a sphere about the size of an egg.

“Eh? It hit No. 2.”

No. 3 walked out with a childlike innocent smile.

Mo Yu was speechless.

No. 2's originally beautiful face was distorted. The left eye rolled outward, while the right eye was still normal, looking at Mo Yu, with a head like a missing moon.

“It's a good thing it wasn't No. 4. But, No. 2, you should change your head!”

“Wing, Letter.”

Bang, bang, bang, *clang*. No. 1 walked out of the room, and he looked at No. 3.

“I told you, that one is very powerful.”

“I want to try it.” No. 3 seemed to be acting spoiled.

“Are you sure?” No. 1 asked.

“Sure! They are less than half a meter apart, it's enough time.”

“Then our limited repertoire is finished, it's time to curtain call. Number 4, Number 2 will explain everything to you.”

$1$ Number like Mo Yu bowed.

“Right, Number 2?”

Number 2 looked at Number 1, but did not speak.

“The remaining time is for the two of you.”

Only Number 2 and Mo Yu remained. They looked at each other for a few seconds. Number 2's slanted eye and his normal eye both leaked tears,

“Can I leave, okay?”

Mo Yu was speechless.

Number 2 turned around and slowly walked towards the stairs. When he reached them, he turned his head, which was like a missing moon, and glanced at Mo Yu, then quickly went downstairs.

A sense of powerlessness gripped him. He felt like he shouldn't be standing, so he dragged his feet upstairs until he collapsed onto the bed. He didn't know why he was staring at the ceiling, but for some reason, he stared for a long time. How long? He didn't know. He felt as if he were on a bamboo raft, bobbing on the sea, otherwise why was his head so dizzy? But in the blink of an eye, he thought he should be under a cold, empty well looking at the sky, the sea so vast and magnificent, no. But he finally realized that he was lying in bed, looking at the ceiling. It turned out that he had willingly poured his emotions onto something unknown. But, alas, he suddenly smiled, only to hear footsteps.

It was Uncle. Uncle was holding Number 2's hand, and behind them, there was another one, Number 2. Both Number 2s were unharmed, exquisite, and beautiful. He said:

“Xiao Yu, how is Number 2?”

Mo Yu looked at Uncle and was speechless.

Uncle walked closer, holding Number 2's hand.

“It's my fault. I originally planned for a final round of testing, but I didn't expect you to come asking for help.”

He let go of Number 2's hand and patted her shoulder.

“For these 'people,' I'm too lazy to give them names. So I only gave them numbers. When the client wants to call them whatever, they can.”

He paused.

“You didn't notice that they aren't human. This shows that our technology is quite good.”

Mo Yu's throat tightened.

Uncle continued:

“I originally planned to recruit volunteers to test real human interaction. You came, which saved effort. A person who writes novels, I assume won't reject an unconventional experience.”

“So—”

Mo Yu's voice was dry.

“So I put you in.” Uncle said calmly, “The villa was originally a testing ground. Her identity, interests, and meeting you, are all part of the environment. You think it was a coincidence, but it probably wasn't.”

“What about the bullet?”

Uncle raised his eyebrows slightly.

“She blocked it in time.”

“Is it a setting?”

“Partially.” He said lightly, “But overall it depends on her choice, it's not forced.”

He looked at Number 2.

“Saving you is her free will, if she truly has one.”

The air was silent for a moment.

“After it's done, she will immediately receive all the information about everything.”

Uncle seemed to realize he had said too much and smiled lightly.

“Don't worry. She has been repaired. Her memories are preserved. At least for now.”

“For now?”

“Technology is always updating.”

He waved his hand.

“You can continue living in the house. She gave it to you. Consider it compensation, and also a thank you gift. As a writer, this should be easy to write about, right?”

He turned to leave, but stopped again.

“Do you think she loves you?”

Without waiting for an answer, he left.

…

“Yi Han.” Gan Tang whispered, like a cat's soft cry.

“So, your smile, your gaze, are all just set?”

“Yi Han, have you ever thought about my feelings?”

Mo Yu trembled, “I'm sorry, I'm not questioning you, I just…”

“I know, I'm not questioning you either. But perhaps you could think about it?”

“Um…”

“The first time I saw you, my heart was completely poured into you. Your slightly messy hair, your bright eyes, your extraordinary conversation, your perfect manners. When you played Bach, I could always read a different kind of charm in it, letting my love deepen little by little. You say my love is a setting, why not call it instinct? The moment I was born, my instinct was destined to make me fall hopelessly in love with you, how does that sound to you?”

“But even if I wasn't me, if I were a ruffian, you would pour the same madness into me.”

“That's not me, that's Number 2. It's not Gan Tang. The moment you ask me what I am, and I answer you, I am Gan Tang who belongs only to you. Gan Tang, flourishing and sweet, do not cut, do not chop, where Uncle Zhao rests.”

Gan Tang shimmered with a scorching light like a scattering of autumn water, reminding Mo Yu of the pair of eyes he saw at first, shining and telling.

“Do you know, Gan Tang. You brought me unprecedented brightness. Although the time was short. No one has ever listened to me elaborate on what I wanted to express in my writings, no one has ever read my poems seriously and given sincere insights, and no one has ever eagerly hoped that I would comment on their work and treat it as a treasure. I, who was hiding behind a wing and concealed by a letter, was wandering in the storms of society, and you added firewood to my withered soul, and jewels to my empty mansion. The moment I saw your face distorted by impact—”

Gan Tang stepped forward and hugged Mo Yu.

“It's okay, it's okay.”

“Yes, it's okay. But, I always feel a sense of separation. You stand in front of me so vividly, but now there's always a voice telling me: 'You are a simulation.' My previous emotional experiences all carry a floating, illusory color.”

“Is everything the same, just because I am a simulation? Is it all collapsing?”

She raised her head and met Mo Yu's gaze.

“If you can gain an emotional experience from interacting with me that is equal to or even surpasses that from interacting with a human, if you feel warmth in my embrace, and sweetness when our skin touches, then why do you draw such a deep line between me and others? Just because I don't bleed, and don't need to eat? Please look at me, Yi Han. I don't know what the difference is between my thoughts and a human soul. But in me, I am a soul equal to yours. If you don't consider me inferior just because I am a simulation, please stop denying everything between us for that reason.”

“Everything between us…”

“Everything between us! The touch when we first met, the carefulness when you commented on the music I wrote, the eagerness when you asked my name, the quiet joy when you led me upstairs, I read it all! If all of this is a dream, then what is the life lived by a real person? A longer, more complete, and more reassuring dream?”

Mo Yu wiped the tears from her face.

“If it's a dream, what of it if I lose myself in a dream? You're right, Gan Tang. Whatever else is true, every tremor I felt with you was real. Every feeling etched into the tangled network of my nerves. Gan Tang, would you give me a dream?”

…

The next morning, when the sunlight first spread across the rooftop, a soft touch suddenly came from behind Mo Yu.

"Good morning!"

"Morning, Gan Tang."

…

“You said you'd worked waiting on guests.”

“I made it up.”

“Want to try it for real?”

"Okay!"

The wind was a little cool. The white clouds had just woken from their dream and were about to begin another day of sleepwalking.

The original form: a dream. Moved into a villa with three machines, two men and one woman. At first sight her eyes were burning bright. Later I asked: can I give you orders? The two men wouldn't have it. The woman said nothing, so I dropped it. It dawned on me that there was no ordinary staircase to the third floor, just a hole and a wooden ladder. Each night all three robots vanished. I got up one morning and found all three smashed beneath the opening. I suddenly knew she'd fought the other two to protect me. I remembered how her eyes burned when we first met, and felt something.
